Portfolio Manager 0.5.0: the groundwork for a supplier map.

This version doesn't add a map yet. It adds the engine that will build one, and it ships switched off.

The supply chain pipeline (off by default)
- Settings has a new group, Supply chain pipeline. It's off until you turn on Run pipeline.
- When it's on, it works in the background through a watch list of 59 AI hardware companies (chips, memory, equipment, networking, servers, power and cooling, data centres). It downloads their annual and quarterly reports from the SEC and records the big customers and suppliers that companies state in them, like "Apple is 91% of Cirrus Logic's sales". Every link keeps the page or figure it came from. Companies that only say "Customer A" are kept without a name instead of guessed.
- It uses your own Anthropic key when it reads reports with Claude, so you pay for it, not us. Downloading from the SEC is free. This version mostly collects and counts; it doesn't spend on Claude yet.
- The reports it downloads are kept on your Mac, in the app's data folder: a few hundred megabytes for the whole watch list.
- It has a monthly budget, US$10 to start, which you can change in Settings. When the month's spending reaches it, the pipeline pauses itself and starts again next month.
- "Open pipeline…" shows a sheet with what it's doing: how many watched companies are up to date, how many links it has found, anything that needs attention (with Retry), what it has cost this month, a list of the companies and their filings, and a live log. Pause and Run now are there too.

What's not here yet
- The supplier map itself, where you can see who supplies whom and how that touches your holdings, comes in a later version.
- Companies that aren't listed in the US (Samsung, SK hynix, Tokyo Electron and a few others on the watch list) have no SEC filings, so the pipeline skips them for now.

Also in this version
- Company names in reports are matched to the right company more carefully ("TSMC" and "Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing" are the same company, a subsidiary isn't its parent), and anything unclear is skipped rather than guessed.
